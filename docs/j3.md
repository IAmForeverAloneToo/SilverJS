# J3: the whole language, in five steps

J3 makes SilverJS compile and run all of ECMAScript's syntax
(docs/design.md section 3): iteration and what is built on it, generators,
classes, `async`, `Proxy`, tagged templates and Annex B. Target: 65% of
Test262's measured set run to completion as it says (36.57% at J3's
start, 17,381 of 47,526). Each step lands with its tests, a sabotage
pass, the gate and a push, as J2's did; the micro-benches run at each
step, judged on their median ratio (silverjs-microbench.sh).

**Where the failures are at the start** (test262/expected-run-failures.txt,
by its one-word reason): classes 7,597; generators 3,773; `for-of`
3,516; destructuring 1,649; modules 970 (J5); `async` functions 954;
spread 155; tagged templates 52; and 11,315 that throw for a built-in
not there yet, of which J3 brings `Proxy` (304 of its own, and many
more tests use it), `Promise` (590), the iterator prototypes and Annex
B's semantics (544), and J4 the rest (Temporal, typed arrays, RegExp,
`Date`, `DataView`, `Map`, `Set`, `JSON`...).

**J3.1 Iteration.** The iterator protocol (GetIterator, IteratorStep,
IteratorClose, section 7.4) in the interpreter; `%IteratorPrototype%`,
`%ArrayIteratorPrototype%` with `Array.prototype.values`, `keys`,
`entries` and `@@iterator`, the string and `arguments` iterators;
`for-of` with its iterator closed on `break`, `return` and a throw;
spread in calls, `new` and array literals; destructuring, binding and
assignment, with defaults, holes, rests and computed keys;
`Object.fromEntries`, `Array.from` over iterables. The iterator a
generator gives waits for J3.2; the protocol does not.

As landed, J3.1: the protocol is four instructions over registers an
iteration keeps (its iterator, `next` and a done flag): `GetIterator`,
`IteratorNext` (done set before `next` is called and cleared after a
value, so an iterator whose `next` throws is not closed, and a step past
the end does not call `next` again), and `IteratorClose`, normal or
quiet for a throw. A `for-of` loop's iterator rides on its loop's entry
in the compiler's control stack: a `break` of the loop, or any jump out
past it (`return`, an outer `break` or `continue`), closes it; a throw
out of its body is caught by a handler that closes it quietly and
throws on. An array pattern runs the same way, closing what it did not
exhaust. Assignment targets are evaluated before the value they take
(a member's object and key, then the step or the property read). A
spread call puts its arguments on top of the register file, where a
script callee keeps them as its own: `f(...a)` recurses as deep as
`f(a)`. `Array.prototype.values`, `keys`, `entries` and `@@iterator`,
`String.prototype[@@iterator]`, an arguments object's `@@iterator` and
`Object.fromEntries` join the built-ins; the iterator prototypes' keys,
the arguments object's, and every message (`1 is not iterable`, `Spread
syntax requires ...iterable[Symbol.iterator] to be a function`, `Cannot
destructure property 'a' of 'null' as it is null.`, ...) are Chrome
157's, from a probe of its own. Statements whose body gives no value now
complete with undefined (a script's `1; for (x of []);` is undefined).
Test262 run: 19,542 of 47,526 (41.12%), 2,161 more, none lost; the
expectations file's reasons are now each failure's current one, so
`for-of`, destructuring and spread are gone from it (classes 9,130,
generators 3,909, async 1,047).

**J3.2 Generators.** A generator's frame suspended into the heap at
`yield` and resumed on `next`, `return` and `throw`, its registers and
handlers kept; `yield*` delegating with every completion forwarded;
`%GeneratorFunction%`, `%GeneratorPrototype%`, and generator methods.
From here every iterator of J3.1 may be a generator.

**J3.3 Classes.** Class declarations and expressions: constructors,
`extends` (with `null`), `super()` and `super.x`, home objects,
`new.target`; public and private fields, methods and accessors,
static members and static blocks, `#x in o`; the class's own
`Function.prototype.toString` as the reference browser shows it
(docs/chrome-surface.md), and the stack frames of its methods (J2.5b's
three cases left for classes).

**J3.4 Async.** `Promise` with its job queue (the agent's, run when
the embedder asks and after each script), brought forward from J4
because nothing async runs without it; `async` functions and arrows,
`await` (a suspended frame, as J3.2's), async generators and
`for await`. Test262's `async` tests' `$DONE` already prints through
the runner.

**J3.5 The rest of the language.** `Proxy` with every trap and its
invariants, revocable proxies; tagged templates and their per-site
cached strings arrays; Annex B's syntax and semantics (functions in
blocks in sloppy code, B.3.2's var-scoped bindings, `__proto__` in
literals, HTML comments already lexed, legacy octal escapes already
lexed); `using` declarations wait for J4's `DisposableStack`.
