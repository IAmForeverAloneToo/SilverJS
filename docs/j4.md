# J4: the built-ins, in five steps

J4 gives SilverJS the rest of ECMAScript's library (docs/design.md
section 3): the collections and weak references, binary data, `Date`,
`JSON` and the numbers' formatting, the iterator helpers and the
disposables, and the RegExp engine. Each step lands with its tests, a
sabotage pass, a Chrome probe of its messages where it has any, the
gate and a push, as J3's did; the micro-benches run at each step
(bench-ship, an A/B against the parent when a line moves over 10%).

**Where the failures are at the start** (test262/expected-run-failures.txt
at 2974712: 15,059 of 47,526, 68.31% passing), by directory: Temporal
4,616 (J9); RegExp 1,687; TypedArray and its constructors 2,128;
`language/expressions` 907 and `statements` 301 (mostly for a built-in
missing); Iterator 667; Date 610; modules 594 plus `import` 26 (J5);
DataView 521; Set 383; Object 287; Array 259; String 217; ArrayBuffer
209; Map 204; JSON 166; WeakMap 141; eval code 126; the disposables
(`DisposableStack`, `AsyncDisposableStack`, `SuppressedError`) 219 and
`using` declarations 102; Annex B 147; Promise 93; WeakSet 85; BigInt
77; `Uint8Array` base64 70; FinalizationRegistry 47; WeakRef 29.

**The target.** 90% of the measured set with Temporal set aside, as
J7's bar is: 38,619 of 42,910, the module tests counted against it
(the lead, 2026-11-23; design section 1.7). Temporal (4,616 tests) is
J9's, and with it counted 90% could not be reached at J4. Nothing
ships to sites before J4 is whole, so the order of its steps is the
work's, not the sites'.

**J4.1 Collections and weak references.** `Map`, `Set` with the `Set`
methods (`union`, `intersection`, `difference`, `symmetricDifference`,
`isSubsetOf`, `isSupersetOf`, `isDisjointFrom`), `Map.groupBy`, their
iterators, live across deletion as the spec has them; `WeakMap`,
`WeakSet`, `WeakRef` and `FinalizationRegistry` with the collector:
ephemerons marked to a fixed point, weak targets cleared, cleanup jobs
queued after a collection. Symbols as weak keys where they may be.

As landed, J4.1: every collection keeps its entries in one ordered hash
table (src/table.rs), an internal cell (boxed: unboxed, it grew every
internal cell, closures' environments among them, and an A/B showed
`array_push_index` 10% slower): entries in insertion order, a
deleted one a hole, a chained index by SameValueZero over the entries'
hashes (a string's its cached hash), the holes squeezed out once they
outnumber the live entries. An iterator (and `forEach`, and the set
methods, through hidden ones) holds an index into the table; the table
lists its iterators unheld, and a compaction moves each to its entry's
new place. The collector marks a weak table without its entries, then
marks a value whose key it kept, to a fixed point (ephemerons); a dead
key's entry is removed, a registry's held value moved to its cleared
list and a cleanup job queued after the collection. `WeakRef` is a weak
table of one target; what `new WeakRef` and `deref` touch stays alive
until the script's jobs end. `getOrInsert` and `getOrInsertComputed`
come too: the reference browser ships them. Messages name objects as
the reference browser does (`#<Map>` by the constructor, `[object
Array]` by the tag, `Error: message`), wherever a message shows one.
The Chrome 157 collections probe (keys, behaviour, 46 messages)
matches but for one message, a map iterator's name, which needs
`Iterator.prototype.constructor` (J4.4); a test pins the rest.
Test262 run: 33,388 of 47,526 (70.25%), 921 more, none lost; what is
left of the collections' directories needs BigInt (J4.3).

**J4.2 Binary data.** `ArrayBuffer` (resizable, transferable, detached),
`SharedArrayBuffer` without `Atomics`' waiting, the eleven typed arrays
over `%TypedArray%` with their integer-indexed exotic objects, out of
bounds and length tracking, `DataView` with its getters and setters,
`Uint8Array`'s base64 and hex; external stores for the embedder (J5
uses them for wasm). In two commits: J4.2a the buffers and typed
arrays, J4.2b `DataView`, `SharedArrayBuffer`, `Atomics` and base64
and hex. A fuzz target, `silverjs_buffers`, lands with J4.2a (the
lead's condition): resizable and detached buffers are where engines
are attacked.

As landed, J4.2a: an `ArrayBuffer` holds its bytes in an internal cell
(src/buffer.rs), counted against the heap's limit (past it, the
reference browser's "Array buffer allocation failed"); detaching
empties it. A typed array is its buffer, element type, byte offset
and a length, or none when it tracks a resizable buffer's; its length
is worked out at each access, so that one shrunk past or detached is
out of bounds at once. The integer-indexed exotic object is the heap's:
a canonical numeric key (`1`, `-0`, `1.5`, `NaN`) is an element or
nothing, never a property, nor looked up the prototype chain; a write
of a primitive converts there, one of an object goes back to the agent
for its `valueOf`, and the element is written only if still in bounds
after it. Elements are little-endian; `Float16Array` rounds once from
the double, ties to even. The methods read what they need and check
again after script ran (`fill`, `slice`, `copyWithin`, `set`), as the
spec has them; a method's errors are the reference browser's (the
Chrome 157 binary data probe: keys, 55 behaviours, 81 messages; a test
pins J4.2a's, `JSON.stringify` stood in for until J4.3). The
two BigInt arrays wait for `BigInt` (J4.3). `silverjs_buffers` makes a
script of the bytes: views of every type over resizable and fixed
buffers, their methods called while `valueOf`, getters, comparators,
callbacks and iteration resize, detach and grow the buffers; it checks
that only TypeErrors and RangeErrors come out, that a detached view
reads as empty and no view reaches past its buffer, and never panics.
DataView joins it with J4.2b. The Test262 runner's `$262` has
`detachArrayBuffer` (through the embedder's `detach_array_buffer`);
the immutable buffers proposal, which the reference browser does not
ship, is not applicable. The collector no longer collects at every
allocation when one request is over half the live heap: the next
collection now waits for growth past that request. Test262 run:
34,873 of 47,472 (73.46%), 1,485 more, none lost, 54 immutable-buffer
tests set aside; what is left of the typed arrays' directories needs
`BigInt` or `DataView`.

As landed, J4.2b: a `DataView` is its buffer, byte offset and a byte
length, or none when it tracks a resizable buffer's, checked at each
access as a typed array is; its getters and setters read and write any
element type in either byte order (the BigInt ones come with `BigInt`).
A `SharedArrayBuffer` is an `ArrayBuffer`'s data block that is never
detached and grows but never shrinks; an agent shares it with no other
yet. The reference browser has it only on a cross-origin isolated page
(the shared memory probe, Chrome 157, served with COOP and COEP: keys,
28 behaviours, 78 messages), so SilverJS puts it on a global object, last,
only when `AgentOptions::shared_memory` asks; Silver does not, and its
Test262 tests stay not applicable. `Atomics` every page has: its
operations are plain reads and writes of the integer arrays (one agent),
`wait` throws as the main thread's does, and `waitAsync`'s waiters
are the agent's until `notify` wakes them in order (a finite timeout
needs the host's timers, J5). The Atomics-only tests, 138, now count:
the measured set grows to 47,610, and those of them that use a
`SharedArrayBuffer` or `BigInt64Array` without saying so fail.
`Uint8Array`'s base64 and hex decode as far as the array goes, writing
what they read before an error. The reference browser's own: a
`DataView`'s `byteLength` and `byteOffset` throw out of bounds where six
Test262 tests want 0, and `Atomics` throws a TypeError, not a
RangeError, for an element a conversion shrank away
(docs/chrome-surface.md). `silverjs_buffers` gains data views, shared
buffers, `Atomics` and the decoders. The embedder's external stores
move to J5, with their first user (wasm memories). Test262 run: 35,519
of 47,610 (74.60%), 646 more, none lost.

**J4.3 Dates, JSON and numbers.** `Date` in full with the spec's
parsing and Chrome's fallback forms (a probe), the local time zone
through a hook (UTC until J5 wires Silver's); `JSON.parse` with its
reviver and source text access, `JSON.stringify` with every case and
`rawJSON`; `Number.prototype.toString(radix)`, `toFixed`,
`toExponential`, `toPrecision` exact; the rest of `Math` and `BigInt`.
In four commits: J4.3a `JSON` and `Math.sumPrecise`, J4.3b `BigInt`,
J4.3c the BigInt typed arrays (with `DataView`'s and `Atomics`' BigInt
parts), J4.3d `Date` (the number methods were exact already, J2).

As landed, J4.3a: `JSON.parse` scans the text with a stack of its own,
not the Rust one, making each object and array as it opens; with a
reviver it keeps a record of each value's source text, and the reviver
is given `{ source }` for a primitive still the one parsed (the JSON
parse source text access proposal). `JSON.stringify` likewise walks
with its own stack, so a 100,000-deep text or object needs no native
stack; a cycle is found by a set of the objects open and reported as
the reference browser reports it, the path between drawn with its
first two links and its last. `JSON.rawJSON` makes a frozen,
null-prototype object `stringify` writes as its text. A refused text
has the reference browser's message, the token quoted with ten
characters of context either side and its line and column (the
numbers probe, Chrome 158, run in two time zones). `Math.sumPrecise`
adds its numbers exactly in a fixed-point accumulator 2,240 bits wide
and rounds once, ties to even. Test262 run: 35,756 of 47,610 (75.10%),
237 more, none lost; what is left of `JSON` and `Math` needs `BigInt`
or RegExp literals.

As landed, J4.3b: a BigInt is SilverJS's own integer (src/bigint.rs), a
sign and 32-bit limbs in an arena of the heap's; schoolbook products,
Karatsuba's past 48 limbs, Knuth's division, and the bitwise operators
on the infinite two's complement. Its size cap is the reference
browser's, bisected on Chrome 158: 2^30 - 64 bits, and refused as it
refuses (an addition of magnitudes when an operand already has the
most 64-bit digits, a product when its factors' digits come to more,
`~` never). Every loop that grows with an operand's size spends fuel
and calls the embedder's interrupt hook every 2^22 limb operations, so
`10n ** 10000000n`, a huge `toString` or `BigInt(string)` stop as a
runaway script does (a test interrupts each). `silverjs_bigint`, a
differential fuzz target, checks every operator, `asIntN`, `asUintN`
and the radix conversions against num-bigint, a test-only oracle. The
operators, `==` with strings and numbers, the relational operators,
`Map` and `Set` keys and the conversions take BigInts as the spec has
them, with the reference browser's messages (the numbers probe). The
BigInt typed arrays, `DataView`'s and `Atomics`' BigInt parts are
J4.3c's. Test262 run: 36,072 of 47,610 (75.77%), 316 more, none lost.

As landed, J4.3c: `BigInt64Array` and `BigUint64Array` take their
place among the typed arrays (between `Int32Array` and
`Uint8ClampedArray`, the reference browser's order). An element is a
Number or a BigInt element's 64 bits, written as ToBigInt's low 64
and read back as a BigInt the agent makes: the heap, which cannot
allocate where it reads a property, hands a BigInt element's `[[Get]]`
and own property to the agent. A typed array made from, set from or
species-created for one of the other content type is refused with the
reference browser's TypeError, the methods convert and compare by
content type (a signed or unsigned sort, a search only for a BigInt in
the element type's range), and each BigInt made while others are held
is rooted first (a test under collection stress). `DataView` gains
`getBigInt64`, `setBigInt64`, `getBigUint64` and `setBigUint64`;
`Atomics` works on both arrays, 64 bits wide, and `wait` and
`waitAsync` on a `BigInt64Array`. A typed array of 2^35 bytes or more
is refused as a length, as the reference browser refuses it (the
bigtyped probe, Chrome 158: keys, 10 behaviours, 32 messages). Test262
run: 37,070 of 47,610 (77.86%), 998 more, none lost.

As landed, J4.3d: a date's time value and its arithmetic are
src/date.rs's (days by whole 400-year eras, MakeDay, MakeTime, MakeDate,
TimeClip); the clock and the local time zone are the embedder's
(`Agent::set_clock`, and `Agent::set_time_zone` with a `TimeZone` that
gives the offset and the zone's name at a time value), the system clock
and UTC without them, and nothing else in the engine asks the system.
Local time to UTC takes the earlier instant of a repeated hour and,
in a skipped one, the offset from before the change. `toString` and
its kin write the reference browser's forms, the offset in whole
minutes (`GMT-0456` for New York's mean time); the `toLocale*` forms
are its en-US forms until `Intl` comes (docs/chrome-surface.md).
`Date.parse` reads the Date Time String Format, and past it the forms
the reference browser reads, fitted to probes run on Chrome 158 in UTC
and in New York: 439 real-world strings (RFC 2822, numeric orders,
month names, two-digit years, zone names and offsets, AM and PM,
comments, the engine's own forms, garbage), every ASCII symbol and
some characters past it around a date, and the space characters;
SilverJS gives what it gave for every one. `silverjs_date` fuzzes the
parser against that table and for bounded time and round trips.
`toTemporalInstant` comes with Temporal (J9). Test262 run: 37,847 of
47,610 (79.49%), 777 more, none lost; of `Date`'s, Temporal's eight and
four that need RegExp literals are left.

**J4.4 Iterator helpers, disposables and the remainders.** `Iterator`
and its helpers (`map`, `filter`, `take`, `drop`, `flatMap`, `reduce`,
`toArray`, `forEach`, `some`, `every`, `find`, `Iterator.from`,
`Iterator.concat`), `DisposableStack`, `AsyncDisposableStack`,
`SuppressedError`, `using` and `await using`; what is left of `Array`,
`Object`, `String`, `Function`, `Promise` and Annex B's built-ins
(`escape`, `unescape`, the HTML methods, `Date`'s `getYear`). In three
commits: J4.4a the iterator helpers, J4.4b the disposables with `using`
and `await using`, J4.4c the remainders.

SilverJS ships what the reference browser ships, no more (the lead,
2026-11-25): sites test for a method before they use it, so one Chrome
lacks changes their code paths. The J4.4 probe (Chrome 158) found two
proposals Test262 tests that Chrome does not ship, `Iterator.prototype`'s
`chunks` and `windows` and `Promise.allKeyed` and `allSettledKeyed`;
they are not exposed, and their tests (features `iterator-chunking` and
`await-dictionary`) are not applicable. At each new Chrome major
version the not-applicable proposals are probed again, and one Chrome
has shipped comes back as work.

As landed, J4.4a: `Iterator` (abstract: only a subclass constructs),
`%IteratorPrototype%`'s helpers with `join`, `includes` and
`[Symbol.dispose]` (all the reference browser has), the `constructor`
and `[Symbol.toStringTag]` accessors that refuse to change the
prototype's own, `Iterator.from` and its wrapper, `concat`, `zip` and
`zipKeyed` with their three modes and padding. A helper is a native
object with a generator's states: `next` while it runs is refused, a
`return` before the first `next` closes only what it iterates, one
after it closes what is open (`flatMap`'s inner iterator first, `zip`'s
from the last) while the helper counts as running. The messages are
the reference browser's (the J4.4 probe and its messages probe). Two
rooting slips the collector's verifier found under stress (`zip`'s
padding while its helper was made, a `zipKeyed` iterator while its key
was) are fixed, and the stress test that found them stays. Test262
run: 38,430 of 47,439 pass (81.01%), 754 fewer failures and none new,
with 1,358 not applicable; the three `Iterator.from` tests left need
the Temporal harness, which needs `RegExp` (J4.5).

**J4.5 RegExp.** The engine of design section 1.6: a backtracking
matcher compiled from the pattern, memoized so that no pattern takes
exponential time on a string, with every flag (`d`, `g`, `i`, `m`, `s`,
`u`, `v`, `y`), named groups, lookbehind, Unicode properties and sets;
`RegExp.prototype`'s methods and `String`'s that use them, `matchAll`'s
iterator, `RegExp.escape`, the legacy static properties of Annex B.
The backtracking bound and the fuzz target `silverjs_regexp`
(differential against QuickJS-ng) land with its first commit.
