# J4: the built-ins, in five steps

J4 gives SilverJS the rest of ECMAScript's library (docs/design.md
section 3): the collections and weak references, binary data, `Date`,
`JSON` and the numbers' formatting, the iterator helpers and the
disposables, and the RegExp engine. Each step lands with its tests, a
sabotage pass, a Chrome probe of its messages where it has any, the
gate and a push, as J3's did; the micro-benches run at each step
(bench-ship, an A/B against the parent when a line moves over 10%).

**Where the failures are at the start** (test262/expected-run-failures.txt
at 2974712: 15,059 of 47,526, 68.31% passing), by directory: Temporal
4,616 (J9); RegExp 1,687; TypedArray and its constructors 2,128;
`language/expressions` 907 and `statements` 301 (mostly for a built-in
missing); Iterator 667; Date 610; modules 594 plus `import` 26 (J5);
DataView 521; Set 383; Object 287; Array 259; String 217; ArrayBuffer
209; Map 204; JSON 166; WeakMap 141; eval code 126; the disposables
(`DisposableStack`, `AsyncDisposableStack`, `SuppressedError`) 219 and
`using` declarations 102; Annex B 147; Promise 93; WeakSet 85; BigInt
77; `Uint8Array` base64 70; FinalizationRegistry 47; WeakRef 29.

**The target.** 90% of the measured set with Temporal set aside, as
J7's bar is: 38,619 of 42,910, the module tests counted against it
(the lead, 2026-11-23; design section 1.7). Temporal (4,616 tests) is
J9's, and with it counted 90% could not be reached at J4. Nothing
ships to sites before J4 is whole, so the order of its steps is the
work's, not the sites'.

**J4.1 Collections and weak references.** `Map`, `Set` with the `Set`
methods (`union`, `intersection`, `difference`, `symmetricDifference`,
`isSubsetOf`, `isSupersetOf`, `isDisjointFrom`), `Map.groupBy`, their
iterators, live across deletion as the spec has them; `WeakMap`,
`WeakSet`, `WeakRef` and `FinalizationRegistry` with the collector:
ephemerons marked to a fixed point, weak targets cleared, cleanup jobs
queued after a collection. Symbols as weak keys where they may be.

As landed, J4.1: every collection keeps its entries in one ordered hash
table (src/table.rs), an internal cell (boxed: unboxed, it grew every
internal cell, closures' environments among them, and an A/B showed
`array_push_index` 10% slower): entries in insertion order, a
deleted one a hole, a chained index by SameValueZero over the entries'
hashes (a string's its cached hash), the holes squeezed out once they
outnumber the live entries. An iterator (and `forEach`, and the set
methods, through hidden ones) holds an index into the table; the table
lists its iterators unheld, and a compaction moves each to its entry's
new place. The collector marks a weak table without its entries, then
marks a value whose key it kept, to a fixed point (ephemerons); a dead
key's entry is removed, a registry's held value moved to its cleared
list and a cleanup job queued after the collection. `WeakRef` is a weak
table of one target; what `new WeakRef` and `deref` touch stays alive
until the script's jobs end. `getOrInsert` and `getOrInsertComputed`
come too: the reference browser ships them. Messages name objects as
the reference browser does (`#<Map>` by the constructor, `[object
Array]` by the tag, `Error: message`), wherever a message shows one.
The Chrome 157 collections probe (keys, behaviour, 46 messages)
matches but for one message, a map iterator's name, which needs
`Iterator.prototype.constructor` (J4.4); a test pins the rest.
Test262 run: 33,388 of 47,526 (70.25%), 921 more, none lost; what is
left of the collections' directories needs BigInt (J4.3).

**J4.2 Binary data.** `ArrayBuffer` (resizable, transferable, detached),
`SharedArrayBuffer` without `Atomics`' waiting, the eleven typed arrays
over `%TypedArray%` with their integer-indexed exotic objects, out of
bounds and length tracking, `DataView` with its getters and setters,
`Uint8Array`'s base64 and hex; external stores for the embedder (J5
uses them for wasm).

**J4.3 Dates, JSON and numbers.** `Date` in full with the spec's
parsing and Chrome's fallback forms (a probe), the local time zone
through a hook (UTC until J5 wires Silver's); `JSON.parse` with its
reviver and source text access, `JSON.stringify` with every case and
`rawJSON`; `Number.prototype.toString(radix)`, `toFixed`,
`toExponential`, `toPrecision` exact; the rest of `Math` and `BigInt`.

**J4.4 Iterator helpers, disposables and the remainders.** `Iterator`
and its helpers (`map`, `filter`, `take`, `drop`, `flatMap`, `reduce`,
`toArray`, `forEach`, `some`, `every`, `find`, `Iterator.from`,
`Iterator.concat`), `DisposableStack`, `AsyncDisposableStack`,
`SuppressedError`, `using` and `await using`; what is left of `Array`,
`Object`, `String`, `Function`, `Promise` and Annex B's built-ins
(`escape`, `unescape`, the HTML methods, `Date`'s `getYear`).

**J4.5 RegExp.** The engine of design section 1.6: a backtracking
matcher compiled from the pattern, memoized so that no pattern takes
exponential time on a string, with every flag (`d`, `g`, `i`, `m`, `s`,
`u`, `v`, `y`), named groups, lookbehind, Unicode properties and sets;
`RegExp.prototype`'s methods and `String`'s that use them, `matchAll`'s
iterator, `RegExp.escape`, the legacy static properties of Annex B.
The backtracking bound and the fuzz target `silverjs_regexp`
(differential against QuickJS-ng) land with its first commit.
